<P STYLE="font: 14pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify; color: #0F2B89"><B>A. Regulatory Updates and
News</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;151/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>July 8<SUP>th</SUP>, 2024</B>, the
Secretariat of Energy cancelled the so called Terconf, a thermal generation tender aiming to increase the thermal power supply and its
reliability. Central Puerto had been awarded with 516 MW allocated in Central Costanera.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;193/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>August 1<SUP>st</SUP>, 2024</B>, the
Secretariat of Energy updated remuneration prices for energy and power of generation units not committed in a PPA (spot market). Remuneration
values increased 3% since August 1<SUP>st</SUP>, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Extension of Operation for Piedra del
&Aacute;guila Hydroelectric Complex</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>August 12, 2024</B>, Presidential
decree No. 718/2024 was published in the Official Gazette, extending Central Puerto's concession to operate Piedra del &Aacute;guila Hydroelectric
Complex for one more year. The decree also stated that within 180 days after its issuance date, the Secretary of Energy would call for
a national and international public tender looking to grant a new 30 year concession for Comahue hydroelectric plants.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;233/2024</B>&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>August 29 <SUP>th</SUP>, 2024</B>,
the Secretariat of Energy updated remuneration prices for energy and power of generation units not committed in contracts (spot market).
The resolution replaced Annexes I to V of Resolution N&deg;193/2024 and increased remuneration values by 5% starting September 1st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;285/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>September 27 <SUP>th</SUP>, 2024</B>,
the Secretariat of Energy updated remuneration prices for energy and power of generation units not committed in a PPA (spot market). This
resolution replaced Annexes I to V of Resolution N&deg;233/2024 and established a 2,7% increase in remuneration values effective from
October 1st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;294/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>October 1st, 2024, </B>the Secretariat
of Energy published Resolution N&deg;294 in the Official Gazette, establishing a contingency plan for the electricity sector aiming to
mitigate possible critical situations (&ldquo;<I>Contingency Plan for critical months of the period 2024/2026</I>&rdquo;). The plan covers
the period December 2024 &ndash; March 2026 and states action plans for generation, transmission and distribution, as well as for large
users demand. Regarding generation, an &ldquo;<I>additional, complementary and exceptional</I>&rdquo; remuneration for power and energy
is stablished, with the aim of ensuring the availability of equipment in critical months and hours. The scheme is for thermal power plants
located in critical nodes that do not have MEM supply contracts (PPAs) and that have not adhered to Resolution 59/23 (for combined cycles).
Generators included in this universe are invited to adhere to a &ldquo;<I>Power Availability Commitment and Reliability Improvement</I>&rdquo;
(the Commitment). The Commitment establishes an Availability Price Agreement (USD/MW 2,000) that is affected by a node criticality factor,
which can vary between 0.75 and 1.25, being the final remuneration obtained by the generator affected by the real availability of the
generation units.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">The units belonging to the Group that are
eligible to adhere to this resolution are the steam turbines located in Buenos Aires and Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo, the gas turbines located
in Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo and the Brigadier L&oacute;pez thermal power plant. For Central Puerto, the additional remuneration varies from
USD/MW 2,000 to USD/MW 2,500 depending on months and units considered.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;20/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>November 1st, 2024</B>, the Secretariat
of Energy and Mining published Resolution N&deg;20/2024 in the Official Gazette. This resolution updated the remuneration values for power
and energy generation of units not committed in contracts. It replaced Annexes I to V of Resolution N&deg;285/2024 and established a 6%
increase in remuneration values effective from November 1st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><FONT STYLE="color: #0F2B89"><B>Installed
Power Generation Capacity: </B></FONT>By the end of the third quarter of 2024 (3Q24), the country&rsquo;s installed capacity reached 42,919
MW, which means a decrease of 1% (or 533 MW) compared to the 43,452 MW recorded as of September 30th, 2023. The variation results from
the installation of new power facilities, a reduction in installed capacity and adjustments to the actual installed capacity of certain
machines. The contraction of 533 MW is decomposed as follows: (i) +902 MW of renewable sources, of which +621 MW corresponds to wind farms
(271 MW of new plants installed during the 3Q24), +273 MW to solar plants (76 MW of new capacity installed during the 3Q24) and +8 MW
to biogas power plants; (ii) a reduction of 1,195 MW in hydraulic sources and (iii) a net decrease of 240 MW in thermal sources, where
(295 MW) corresponds to gas turbines, (470 MW) to steam turbines and (99 MW) to diesel engines, being all partially offset by +624 MW
in combined cycles. The decline of 1,195 MW in hydro installed capacity is basically explained by a reassessment of Yacyret&aacute;&rsquo;s
power available for Argentina and Paraguay. Since August 2024, 50% of Yacyreta&rsquo;s installed capacity is allocated to Argentina, whereas
it used to be approximately 88% before then.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><FONT STYLE="color: #0F2B89"><B>Power generation
&amp; demand </B></FONT>: During the 3Q24, energy demand increased 1% to 35,635 GWh compared to 35,195 GWh recorded during the 3Q23, which
is basically explained by a rise in residential consumption due to weather conditions. Colder temperatures during July and August of this
year <I>vis-&agrave;-vis</I> same months of 2023 prompted higher retail consumption, which shrank in September as a result of milder temperatures
compared to equal month of 2023. End to end, residential consumption rose 3%. With regards to major demand, a slightly higher consumption
was recorded, specially for wholesale major demand, which was boosted by stronger economic activity. The trend is similar to residential
pattern: positive YoY growth rates during July and August then decreasing in September.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On the other hand, generation decreased 3% during
the quarter on a year-over-year (YoY) basis. The decrease was driven by hydro generation (-33%). Renewable and thermal generation both
rose 13%, and nuclear generation grew 7%.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Hydro generation shrank due to a combination
of two factors: i) the aforementioned change in the allocation of Yacyret&aacute;&rsquo;s installed capacity and energy generation upon
Paraguay&rsquo;s claim and ii) a reduction of river flows. In this regard, the contraction was as follows: 60% in Neuqu&eacute;n River,
55% in the Coll&oacute;n Cur&aacute; River, 38% in the Limay River, 26% in the Uruguay River and 22% in the Paran&aacute; River. As it
was previously stated, Paraguay historically consumed a smaller portion of the energy produced at Yacyret&aacute;: while this country
took only 15% of the generated energy in 2023, this year it started to take its full 50% share, leaving Argentina with a smaller portion
of the generated energy.</P>
<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 0">Nuclear supply growth 7% basically as a result of higher availability
and generation of Atucha II. This performance was partially offset by Embalse Power Plant, which entered into maintenance shutdown in
September and resumed operations in October.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">The increase in energy generation from renewable
sources was driven mainly by the impact of new installed capacity.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Finally, there was an increase in thermal
generation to cope with higher energy demand. Despite the slight decrease in availability (71% on average during the 3Q24 vs 73% on average
during the same period of 2023), generation rose 13% YoY. The growth in thermal generation led to higher fuel consumption: 66% of gas
oil, 9% of natural gas and 3% fuel oil. The breakdown of availability levels shows that combined cycles figures increased 4%, from 90%
to 94%, while gas and steam turbines decreased 4%, from 61% to 57%.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Additionally, the electricity trade balance
resulted in a net import situation during the whole quarter, with a peak in August: YoY, net import values were 32% higher in July and
339% higher in August while it was significantly reduced in September.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify; color: #0F2B89"><B>During 3Q24, Central Puerto&rsquo;s
power generation slightly decreased 1% to 5,685 GWh, compared to 5,721 GWh in 3Q23. </B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">This minor decline was influenced by various
factors across different energy sources.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Hydro energy generation from Piedra del Aguila
drop 35%, reaching 1,405 GWh from 2,151 GWh in 3Q23. This decline was primarily due to a 55% reduction in water levels of the Coll&oacute;n
Cur&aacute; River, which resulted in lower availability of water for generation.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Regarding renewable generation, there are mixed
results. Wind generation decreased 4%, reaching 386 GWh in 3Q24 compared to 404 GWh in the same period of 2023. This decline was mainly
due to lower wind resource and also to some maintenance works, including those performed in several blades of La Castellana II, which
were damaged by a storm in December 2023. On the other hand, solar energy generation reached 61 GWh in 3Q24 with full impact during the
quarter since there is no comparable data for 3Q23.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Thermal generation increased 21% during 3Q24
compared to 3Q23, reaching 3,832 GWh from 3,166 GWh. The growth was mainly due to higher dispatch of some units at Puerto site and higher
availability and dispatch at Costanera site. Cogeneration units in Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo and Brigadier Lopez open cycle also had higher
availability and dispatch.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Finally, steam production rose 77% during 3Q24,
reaching 880 thousands of tons compared to 498 in 3Q23. This growth was driven by a 124% rise at San Lorenzo cogeneration plant and a
25% growth at Lujan de Cuyo facility. The surge at Lujan de Cuyo was primarily due to higher availability of gas turbines, following the
completion of a maintenance program in mid-2023, and increased demand from YPF. A higher demand from San Lorenzo&rsquo;s client was also
recorded.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>EBITDA &amp; ADJUSTED EBITDA </B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">In this release, <B>EBITDA</B>, a non-IFRS financial
measure, is defined as net income for the period, <I>plus</I> finance expenses, <I>minus</I> finance income, <I>minus </I>share of the
profit (loss) of associates, <I>plus (minus) </I>losses (gains) on net monetary position, <I>plus</I> income tax expense, <I>plus</I>
depreciation and amortization, <I>minus</I> net results of discontinued operations.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Adjusted EBITDA</B> refers to EBITDA <I>excluding
</I>impairment on property, plant &amp; equipment, foreign exchange difference and interests related to FONI trade receivables and variations
in fair value of biological asset.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Adjusted EBITDA is believed to provide useful
supplemental information to investors about the Company and its results. Adjusted EBITDA is among the measures used by the Company&rsquo;s
management team to evaluate the financial and operating performance and make day-to-day financial and operating decisions. In addition,
Adjusted EBITDA is frequently used by securities analysts, investors, and other parties to evaluate companies in the industry. Adjusted
EBITDA is believed to be helpful to investors because it provides additional information about trends in the core operating performance
prior to considering the impact of capital structure, depreciation, amortization, and taxation on the results.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered in isolation
or as a substitute for other measures of financial performance reported in accordance with IFRS. Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as an
analytical tool, including:</P>
