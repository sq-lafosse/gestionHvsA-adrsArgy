<P STYLE="font: 11pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin-top: 6pt; margin-right: 0; margin-bottom: 6pt; text-align: justify">Buenos
Aires, <B>March 11<SUP>th</SUP></B>, Central Puerto S.A (&ldquo;Central Puerto&rdquo; or the &ldquo;Company&rdquo;) (NYSE: CEPU), the
largest private sector power generation companies in Argentina, reports its consolidated financial results for the <B>Fiscal Year 2024</B>
and <B>Fourth Quarter 2024 (</B>&ldquo;4Q24&rdquo;), ended on December 31st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 11pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">A conference call to discuss the <B>FY 2024</B>
and <B>4Q24 results </B>will be held on <B>March 12<SUP>th</SUP>, 2025, at 9 AM Eastern Time</B> (see details below). All information
provided is presented on a consolidated basis, unless otherwise stated.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 11pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Financial statements as of <B>December 31st,
2024, </B>include the effects of the inflation adjustment, applying IAS 29. Accordingly, the financial statements have been stated in
terms of the measuring unit current at the end of the reporting period, including the corresponding financial figures for previous periods
reported for comparative purposes. Growth comparisons refer to the same periods of the previous year, measured in the current unit at
the end of the period, unless otherwise stated. Consequently, the information included in the Financial Statements for the fiscal year
ended on <B>December 31st, 2024, </B>is not comparable to the Financial Statements previously published by the company. However, we presented
some figures converted from Argentine Pesos to U.S. dollars for comparison purposes only. The exchange rate used to convert Argentine
Pesos to U.S. dollars was the reference exchange rate (Communication &ldquo;A&rdquo; 3500) reported by the Central Bank for U.S. dollars
for the end of each period. The information presented in U.S. dollars is for the convenience of the reader only and may defer if such
conversion for each period is performed at the exchange rate applicable at the end of the latest period. You should not consider these
translations to be representations that the Argentine Peso amounts actually represent these U.S. dollars amounts or could be converted
into U.S. dollars at the rate indicated.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 11pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Definitions and terms used herein are provided
in the Glossary at the end of this document. This release does not contain all the Company&rsquo;s financial information. As a result,
investors should read this release in conjunction with Central Puerto&rsquo;s consolidated financial statements as of and for the fiscal
year ended on <B>December 31st, 2024, </B>and the notes thereto, which will be available on the Company&rsquo;s website.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 14pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify; color: #0F2B89"><B>A. Regulatory Updates and
News</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B><BR>
Resolution SE N&deg;285/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>September 27<SUP>th</SUP>, 2024</B>,
the Secretariat of Energy updated remuneration prices for energy and power of generation units not committed in a PPA (spot market).
This resolution replaced Annexes I to V of Resolution N&deg;233/2024 and established a 2,7% increase in remuneration values effective
from October 1st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;294/2024</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>October 1st, 2024, </B>the Secretariat
of Energy published Resolution N&deg;294 in the Official Gazette, establishing a contingency plan for the electricity sector aiming to
mitigate possible critical situations (&ldquo;<I>Contingency Plan for critical months of the period 2024/2026</I>&rdquo;). The plan covers
the period December 2024 &ndash; March 2026 and states action plans for generation, transmission and distribution, as well as for large
users demand. Regarding generation, an &ldquo;<I>additional, complementary and exceptional</I>&rdquo; remuneration for power and energy
is stablished, with the aim of ensuring the availability of equipment in critical months and hours. The scheme is for thermal power plants
located in critical nodes that do not have MEM supply contracts (PPAs) and that have not adhered to Resolution 59/23 (for combined cycles).
Generators included in this universe are invited to adhere to a &ldquo;<I>Power Availability Commitment and Reliability Improvement</I>&rdquo;
(the Commitment). The Commitment establishes an Availability Price Agreement (USD/MW 2,000) that is affected by a node criticality factor,
which can vary between 0.75 and 1.25, being the final remuneration obtained by the generator affected by the real availability of the
generation units.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">The units belonging to the Group that are
eligible to adhere to this resolution are the steam turbines located in Buenos Aires and Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo, the gas turbines located
in Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo and the Brigadier L&oacute;pez thermal power plant. For Central Puerto, the additional remuneration varies from
USD/MW 2,000 to USD/MW 2,500 depending on months and units considered.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">&nbsp;</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Resolution SE N&deg;20/2024</B></P>

of Energy published Resolution N&deg;20/2024 in the Official Gazette. This resolution updated the remuneration values for power and energy
generation of units not committed in contracts. It replaced Annexes I to V of Resolution N&deg;285/2024 and established a 6% increase
in remuneration values effective from November 1st, 2024.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Puna power transmission line</B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On <B>December 5th, 2024</B>, Central Puerto
signed an agreement with the International Finance Corporation (IFC) to jointly finance the feasibility studies of a power transmission
line to supply energy to mining companies in northwestern Argentina. These studies will evaluate the technical, economic and environmental
feasibility of the project, which aims to interconnect mining projects in the Argentine Puna region to the Argentine Interconnection System
(SADI), guaranteeing a reliable supply of renewable energy through private agreements.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Later, on <B>January 14th, 2025</B>, the
Company and YPF Luz signed an agreement to jointly move forward with the analyses and development of this strategic project. This agreement
marks an important milestone, as it is the first time that two major electricity generation companies will jointly evaluate the technical
and regulatory aspects necessary to carry out a large-scale electrical infrastructure project that will provide a comprehensive electricity
supply solution, with a particular focus on the development of the mining industry. This joint effort would involve an investment ranging
from $250 million to $400 million, depending on the final scope of the project. The development under evaluation includes the construction
of approximately 140 km of electric power transmission line, which could potentially be extended up to 350 km.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><FONT STYLE="color: #0F2B89"><B>Installed
Power Generation Capacity: </B></FONT>By the end of the fourth quarter of 2024 (4Q24), the country&rsquo;s installed capacity reached
43,350 MW, which means a decrease of 1% (or 423 MW) compared to the 43,773 MW recorded as of December 31st, 2023. The variation results
from the installation of new power facilities, a reduction in installed capacity and adjustments/repowering to power plants already in
operations. The contraction of 423 MW is decomposed as follows: (i) +925 MW of renewable sources, of which +614 MW corresponds to wind
farms (224MW of new plants installed during the 4Q24), +307 MW to solar plants (88 MW of new capacity installed during the 4Q24) and
+4 MW to biogas power plants; (ii) a reduction of 1,195 MW in hydraulic sources and (iii) a net decrease of 153 MW in thermal sources,
where a contraction was recorded in gas turbines (-470 MW), steam turbines (-470 MW) and diesel engines (-101 MW), being all partially
offset by +888 MW in combined cycles (130 MW of new capacity installed during the 4Q24). The decline of 1,195 MW in hydro installed capacity
is basically explained by a reassessment of Yacyret&aacute;&rsquo;s power available for Argentina and Paraguay. Since August 2024, 50%
of Yacyreta&rsquo;s installed capacity is allocated to Argentina, whereas it used to be approximately 88% before then.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><FONT STYLE="color: #0F2B89"><B>Power generation
&amp; demand</B></FONT>: During 4Q24, energy demand reached 33,250 GWh, a slight decrease from the 33,258 GWh recorded during the 4Q24.
There was a 1% decrease in residential consumption almost offset by commercial (+1%) and major demand (+1%). Higher temperatures recorded
during October 2024 <I>vis-&agrave;-vis</I> same month of 2023 prompted higher retail consumption, which shrank in November and December
as a result of milder temperatures compared to equal months of 2023.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">For the whole of 2024 residential demand
barely grew 0.4%. Major and commercial demands both ended 2024 with a 1% decrease in their consumption, though some positive interannual
growth rates were observed during the second half of the year for food and beverage, oil &amp; gas and mining.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">On the other hand, generation decreased
2% during the quarter on a year-over-year (YoY) basis. The decrease was driven by nuclear and hydro generation (-48% and -30%, respectively).
Renewable and thermal generation rose 13% and 24%, respectively.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Nuclear generation decreased basically by
the two year-maintenance shutdown of Atucha I, which started in November, an a seasonal maintenance program of Atucha II, carried on between
the end of September and the beginning of December. Hydro generation shrank due to a combination of two factors: i) the aforementioned
change in the allocation of Yacyret&aacute;&rsquo;s installed capacity and energy generation upon Paraguay&rsquo;s claim and ii) a reduction
of river flows. In this last regard, the contraction was as follows: 69% in the Uruguay River, 44% in the Paran&aacute; River, 22% in
the Limay River and 7% in the Coll&oacute;n Cur&aacute; River. As it was previously stated, Paraguay historically consumed a smaller portion
of the energy produced at Yacyret&aacute;: while this country took only 15% of the generated energy in 2023, this year it started to take
its full 50% share, leaving Argentina with a smaller portion of the generated energy.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">The increase in energy generation from renewable
sources was driven mainly by the impact of new installed capacity.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Finally, there was an increase in thermal
generation to cope with the lower supply of hydro and nuclear. Despite the slight decrease in availability (72% on average during the
4Q24 vs 74% on average during the same period of 2023), generation rose 24% YoY. The growth in thermal generation led to higher fuel consumption:
66% rise in gas oil, 9% in natural gas and 3% in fuel oil. The breakdown of availability levels shows that combined cycles figures decreased
2%, from 91% to 89%, while gas and steam turbines decreased 1%, from 63% to 62%.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt/115% Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Additionally, the electricity trade balance
resulted in a net import situation during the whole quarter, with a peak in November: in line with the demand trend showcased above, net
imports were recorded in October and November, being substantially lower in December.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">This operating result is a consequence of several

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Hydro energy generation from Piedra del Aguila
dropped 31%, reaching 1,164 GWh from 1,678 GWh in 4Q23. This decline was primarily due to a 7% reduction in water levels of the Coll&oacute;n
Cur&aacute; River and 22% in the Limay River, which resulted in lower availability of water for generation.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Regarding renewable generation, there are mixed
results. Wind generation decreased 3%, reaching 396 GWh in 4Q24 compared to 410 GWh in the same period of 2023. This decline was mainly
due to lower wind resource and also to some maintenance works. On the other hand, solar energy generation reached 88 GWh in 4Q24 compared
to 73 GWh in the same period of 2023 basically as a result of higher resource availability. This year was the first completely 12-month
period of full operation of this power generation facility.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Thermal generation increased 25% during 4Q24
compared to 4Q23, reaching 3,767 GWh from 3,007 GWh. The growth was mainly due to higher dispatch of some steam turbines in Puerto site
and some steam and gas turbines in Luj&aacute;n de Cuyo, as well as higher generation of the Brigadier Lopez open cycle and the combined
cycle of Santa Fe. Also, a higher availability and dispatch were recorded for the Mitsubishi combined cycle located in Costanera site.
During the quarter some maintenance programs were carried out in steam turbines and combined cycles. It is worth to be mentioned those
executed in the combined cycle located in Nuevo Puerto (Puerto site); one unit of the Mitsubishi combined cycle, located in Costanera
site and some steam turbines of Puerto site.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Finally, steam production rose 71% during 4Q24,
reaching 736 thousand tons compared to 431 in 4Q23. This growth was driven by a 183% rise at San Lorenzo cogeneration plant and a 5% growth
at Lujan de Cuyo facility. The surge at Lujan de Cuyo was primarily due to higher demand from YPF. A higher demand from San Lorenzo&rsquo;s
client was also recorded.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>EBITDA &amp; ADJUSTED EBITDA </B></P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">In this release, <B>EBITDA</B>, a non-IFRS financial
measure, is defined as net income for the period, <I>plus</I> finance expenses, <I>minus</I> finance income, <I>minus </I>share of the
profit (loss) of associates, <I>plus (minus) </I>losses (gains) on net monetary position, <I>plus</I> income tax expense, <I>plus</I>
depreciation and amortization, <I>minus</I> net results of discontinued operations.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify"><B>Adjusted EBITDA</B> refers to EBITDA <I>excluding
</I>impairment on property, plant &amp; equipment, foreign exchange difference and interests related to FONI trade receivables and variations
in fair value of biological asset.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Adjusted EBITDA is believed to provide useful
supplemental information to investors about the Company and its results. Adjusted EBITDA is among the measures used by the Company&rsquo;s
management team to evaluate the financial and operating performance and make day-to-day financial and operating decisions. In addition,
Adjusted EBITDA is frequently used by securities analysts, investors, and other parties to evaluate companies in the industry. Adjusted
EBITDA is believed to be helpful to investors because it provides additional information about trends in the core operating performance
prior to considering the impact of capital structure, depreciation, amortization, and taxation on the results.</P>

<P STYLE="font: 12pt Calibri, Helvetica, Sans-Serif; margin: 6pt 0; text-align: justify">Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered in isolation
or as a substitute for other measures of financial performance reported in accordance with IFRS. Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as an
analytical tool, including:</P>
